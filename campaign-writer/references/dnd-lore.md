# Rooting a D&D adventure in D&D

For D&D (any edition), the adventure should read as if it belongs in the game: creatures act the way their lore says, and names sound like the people they belong to. A kobold called Bartholomew or a dwarf called Gwendolyn breaks the spell before anyone has rolled a die. This file is the checklist. Do it while writing the NPC list in `campaign.json`, and again in the quality-bar loremaster pass.

Facts below were looked up in October 2026; sources at the end. For any species or creature not covered, **look up its lore and name conventions before using it**. Don't guess from the stat block alone.

## 1. Species first, then the name

Give every named NPC an ancestry before you give them a name, and write both in the NPC list. Then pick a name that sounds like that ancestry.

- **Rules edition matters.** The 2024 Player's Handbook has ten species: aasimar, dragonborn, dwarf, elf, gnome, goliath, halfling, human, orc and tiefling. Half-elf and half-orc are not species any more, and the 2024 book dropped the per-species name lists. For a 2024 table, describe such NPCs as human, elf or orc (or say "of mixed heritage" in the text). 2014 and Pathfinder tables can keep half-species.
- **Don't reuse one name style for everyone.** If every NPC is a Germanic or English name, the table can't tell a dwarf from a dockhand. Humans in a port or crossroads can have any mix. Other species should not.

### Name conventions (verified)

| Ancestry | Convention | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Dwarf** | Given name plus clan name. Clan names are compound, craft or stone words | Eldeth, Helja, Kathra, Gurdis, Thorin, Tordek; clans Loderr, Dankil, Ironfist, Frostbeard, Fireforge, Battlehammer |
| **Orc / half-orc** | Short, blunt, one or two syllables, hard consonants. Usually no surname | Dench, Feng, Henk, Holg, Krusk, Mhurren, Thokk; Baggi, Emen, Neega, Sutha, Vola |
| **Halfling** | Melodic given name plus a homely compound family name | Perrin, Merric, Lidda, Seraphina; Goodbarrel, Tealeaf, Underbough, Thorngage |
| **Kobold** | Short, sharp, often one syllable, full of clicks and hisses. Named by the warren for what they do or have done, and they may add to or change a name after an event | Meepo, Sik, Ett, Eks, Irtos, Kashak, Sniv, Taklak, Urak, Varn |
| **Dragon** | A long, grand name in Draconic. Mortals use a shorter or mangled form, which is a nice small story all by itself | (invent: rolling consonants, endings like -ax, -yx, -oth) |

Not verified in this pass: elf, gnome, dragonborn, tiefling, goliath, aasimar and regional human conventions. Look them up before naming; the 2014 Player's Handbook chapter 2 and Volo's Guide to Monsters have tables.

## 2. Creatures behave like their lore

A stat block gives numbers. The lore gives motives. Use the motive.

- **Kobolds.** In the 2024 Monster Manual kobolds are Dragons, not humanoids: small draconic cousins who serve dragons and in some lands worship them as divine. Their scales echo the dragons that live near their warren; metallic features are rare. They are zealous, ingenious, cowardly in a fair fight and brave in a trap. A kobold meeting a dragon should be awestruck, not casual.
- **Copper dragons.** Pranksters, joke-tellers and riddlers; sociable, even-tempered, fond of bards, sulky if their joke dies. Chaotic good, but covetous and jealous. They lair in dry rocky uplands, in narrow caverns behind concealed entrances, with labyrinthine tunnels they compete to make confusing, and they rescue guests who are really lost.
- **Dwarves** keep records and grudges. **Goblins** are small, cowardly in the open and vicious in numbers; hired goblins in costume are funnier than goblins in armour. **Orcs** are not "evil by species" in 2024; play them as people.
- **Any metallic or chromatic dragon:** check its temperament, lair type and attitude to lesser creatures before writing its scene.

If the adventure needs a creature to act against its lore, say why in the text (a curse, a bargain, a trick). Otherwise follow the lore.

## 3. Use the system's own names

- Use SRD stat block names exactly (Goblin Minion, Kobold Warrior, Animated Rug of Smothering, Tough Boss, Swarm of Ravens). Rename tokens, not stat blocks.
- Use the system's own coins, languages, conditions and item names. "Gold piece", "copper piece", "Draconic", "Dwarvish", "potion of healing".
- Page numbers and stat blocks come from the SRD in front of you, not memory.

## 3b. Settings and proper nouns

Named settings, gods, cities and NPCs from published worlds (Forgotten Realms, Eberron and so on) are not in the SRD. Don't put them in a package that will be shared publicly. For a private table the user can ask for them: then use only what you can look up, and say in the README which parts are canon and which are invented. An invented city in an unnamed world is fine; it still follows everything above.

## Checking it

In the quality-bar loremaster pass, list every named NPC and creature with its ancestry and the reason its name fits. Reread each creature's scene against its lore. Fix any mismatch, and report what you looked up and what you didn't.
